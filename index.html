Course overview

The course develops understanding of plant processes at molecular and cellular levels, including major metabolic pathways, photosynthesis, respiration and plant responses to environmental stresses. It also emphasises experimental techniques, data analysis and application of plant-science knowledge to agriculture, conservation and biotechnology.

Planning note: The supplied syllabus allocates 15 lectures to each of six units (90 lectures in total). This page adapts the supplied 15-week work plan into a proposed 12-week teaching sequence as requested. The source does not provide exact semester dates, so calendar dates are marked “To be added”. Confirm weekly contact hours, practical slots and assessment dates with the approved college timetable.

Course units

  1. Unit I — Introduction and Plant Water Relation: history and Indian scientists; water potential; water and solute transport; root absorption; xylem transport; transpiration; stomata; guttation; photoassimilate translocation.
  2. Unit II — Photosynthesis: photosynthetic apparatus, pigments and photosystems; water photo-oxidation; electron/proton transport; Calvin cycle; photorespiration; C4 and CAM pathways; factors affecting photosynthesis; artificial photosynthesis.
  3. Unit III — Respiration and Lipid Metabolism: glycolysis, PPP, TCA cycle, mitochondrial electron transport, ATP synthesis, glyoxylate cycle, lipid structure and function, fatty-acid biosynthesis and oxidation.
  4. Unit IV — Nitrogen Metabolism and Secondary Metabolites: nitrogen uptake and fixation, nodulation, ammonium assimilation, amino-acid biosynthesis; terpenes, phenols and nitrogenous compounds.
  5. Unit V — Plant Hormone and Sensory Photobiology: major plant hormones, their synthesis, transport, effects and mechanisms; phytochromes, cryptochromes, phototropins, photoperiodism and biological clocks.
  6. Unit VI — Seed Science and Stress Physiology: seed development, reserves, germination and dormancy; biotic and abiotic stress; drought, salinity, freezing, heat and oxidative stress.

  1. CO1 — Unit I: Explain and analyse plant water relations, water potential, membrane transport, xylem transport, transpiration, stomatal regulation, guttation and photoassimilate translocation.
  2. CO2 — Unit II: Explain and compare photosynthetic pigments, photosystems, electron/proton transport, carbon-assimilation pathways, photorespiration, C4 and CAM pathways, and factors affecting photosynthesis.
  3. CO3 — Unit III: Analyse major respiratory pathways and energy conversion, including glycolysis, PPP, TCA cycle, mitochondrial electron transport, ATP synthesis and the glyoxylate cycle; explain lipid structure, synthesis and oxidation.
  4. CO4 — Unit IV: Explain nitrogen uptake, fixation, nodulation, ammonium assimilation and amino-acid biosynthesis, and classify plant secondary metabolites by major groups, synthesis and biological roles.
  5. CO5 — Unit V: Compare the biosynthesis, transport, breakdown, physiological effects and mechanisms of action of major plant hormones, and explain photoreceptor function, photoperiodism and biological clocks.
  6. CO6 — Unit VI: Evaluate seed development, reserve mobilisation, germination and dormancy processes, and analyse plant responses and resistance mechanisms under biotic and abiotic stresses.
  7. CO7 — Practical work: Plan or carry out suitable plant-physiology investigations, including seed-germination experiments, using appropriate variables, controls, observations, safety and reproducible recording.
  8. CO8 — Across units: Interpret and communicate plant-physiology evidence using labelled diagrams, charts, data tables and evidence-based explanations, including the contributions of Indian plant scientists and applications in agriculture, conservation or biotechnology.

Practice task: plan a seed-germination investigation

This task is aligned with the Unit VI activity specified in the syllabus: an experimental study of factors affecting seed germination.

Task

Prepare a one-page experimental plan to investigate how one selected factor affects seed germination. State the research question, hypothesis, independent variable, dependent variable, control conditions, number of replicates, observation schedule, data table layout and safety considerations.

Submission checklist

  • Research question and testable hypothesis.
  • Clear identification of variables and a suitable control.
  • Consistent observation schedule and replicate plan.
  • Blank table for recording germinated seeds and total seeds observed.
  • Short note explaining how results will be compared and communicated.

Language support

Use this section to support students in reading scientific terminology, explaining processes and preparing laboratory records. Course-specific bilingual glossary and approved language policy: To be added.

Study and writing supports

  • Keep a glossary with the scientific term, a short definition and a student-written explanation in their preferred study language.
  • For each pathway, practise a three-step explanation: starting material, key process and outcome.
  • Use labelled diagrams before writing a full paragraph about water transport, photosynthesis, respiration or hormone action.
  • For practical records, separate aim, principle, materials, method, observations, result and limitations.
  • When discussing data, describe what was observed before suggesting an explanation; distinguish observation from inference.

Sentence frames for scientific explanations

  • “The purpose of this experiment is to investigate …”
  • “The independent variable is …, while the dependent variable is …”
  • “The results show …; one possible explanation is …”
  • “A limitation of this observation is …”
  • “The evidence supports / does not support the hypothesis because …”
